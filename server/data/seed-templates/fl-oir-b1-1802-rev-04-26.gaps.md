# FL OIR-B1-1802 Uniform Mitigation Verification (Rev. 04/26) — what this template does not fill in

`fl-oir-b1-1802-rev-04-26.json` binds **89 of the 96 blanks** the published field map
(`server/lib/statutory/forms/fl-oir-b1-1802.ts`) names. This file is the other seven, and
the smaller gaps that sit behind the ones that ARE bound.

**It exists because a blank box cannot explain itself.** On a printed statutory form,
"we left this empty deliberately" and "we forgot" are the same picture. Every entry below
says which it is, and what would have to exist before it could be filled.

Measured 2026-08-30, against the authority's own PDF
(sha256 `e02320a4f6d6f5d3f72de07e5a3d2955e9bb3ae97a35576dfcc4b64d04177e5c`) and against a
field map whose every coordinate had already been read back against that page by hand.

|---|---:|
| blanks the field map names | 96 |
| bound by this template | 89 |
| **left unbound** | **7** |
| bindings naming a blank the form does not print | 0 |

## 1. The seven unbound blanks

### 1.1 `inspector_initials` — the header blank on all six pages

The form prints `Inspectors Initials ___________` at the top of every page, beside the
property address. **No source anywhere in this repository.** The nearest thing is
`users.name`, and deriving initials from a name is our arithmetic rather than the
inspector's answer — a person whose legal name and professional initials differ would get
a header they never wrote on an authority's form.

**To close it:** a column on the inspector's profile holding the initials they sign with,
then a new member on `StatutoryInspectionField`, then `{ from: 'inspection', field: … }`
here. It is the same three steps `inspector_license_type` took.

⚠️ It must NOT be closed by binding `{ from: 'item' }`. See §1.3.

### 1.2 `contact_person` — page 1, the box printed `Contact Person:`

The person to contact about the property, printed beside `Owner Name:`. **No source.** The
closed list's nearest members are `client_name` and `owner_name`, and this is neither: the
form prints all three side by side, so the publisher has already said they are different
people.

**To close it:** a column beside the owner block on `statutory_inspection_details`, then a
union member, then an `inspection` binding.

### 1.3 `owner_city`, `owner_zip`, `owner_county` — page 1, the Owner Information block

The form splits the owner's mailing address into four printed blanks — `Address:`,
`City:`, `Zip:`, `County:` — and this product holds it as **one** string,
`statutory_inspection_details.owner_mailing_address`. That column answers the `Address:`
blank; the other three have no source and stay empty.

The owner columns this form needs were enumerated when they were added, and these three
were not among them. That is why they are absent rather than overlooked.

🔴 **They must not be answered with `{ from: 'item' }` or `{ from: 'literal' }`, and the
gate will not stop you.** `binding-policy.ts` refuses a person-shaped field on any route
but `inspection` and `signature`, and it decides "person-shaped" from the FIELD NAME:
`/(^|_)(name|email|phone|address|dob|birth|ssn)(_|$)/i`. `owner_city`, `owner_zip`,
`owner_county` and `contact_person` are every one of them a fact about a named individual
and not one of them matches that pattern. Routing them through an item would put an
identifiable person's postal address into `statutory_form_entries.values`, which is
declared to carry no personal data, with every gate green — the exact failure that file's
own header describes.

**To close them:** three columns beside `owner_mailing_address`, three union members, three
`inspection` bindings. Not a template change.

### 1.4 `homeowner_signature` and `homeowner_signature_date` — page 6

The form prints a second signature block: *Homeowner to complete: I certify that the named
Qualified Inspector, or his or her employee, did perform an inspection of the residence
identified on this form and that proof of identification was provided to me or my
Authorized Representative.*

**This product records no homeowner's mark for an inspection.** `from: 'signature'` is the
only route a signature may take, and `resolveSignatures` answers every scope from
`users.default_signature_base64` — the INSPECTOR's saved mark. Binding either of these
would print one person's signature under a declaration somebody else made, which is worse
than a blank: the blank is a box the homeowner signs by hand, which is what the page is
for.

`homeowner_signature_date` is left with it. A date under an unsigned certification is a
date nobody attested to.

**To close them:** a homeowner signing flow, then a signature scope that resolves to it —
`resolveSignatures` already refuses every scope but `whole_form` by name, so the refusal
is in place and waiting for the source.

## 2. Gaps behind blanks that ARE bound

### 2.1 🔴 `inspector_qualification` is a six-answer question answered from a free-text box

The form prints six checkboxes under *Qualified Inspector – I hold an active license as
a: (check one)*, and the map has a box for each: `home_inspector`,
`building_code_inspector`, `contractor`, `professional_engineer`,
`professional_architect`, `other_recognized_by_insurer`. It is in `requiredFields`.

It is bound to `users.statutory_qualification` — the
qualification is a fact about the inspector, not about the house, so it belongs on the
profile. That column is deliberately **free text**, and the reason is written beside it in
`server/api/profile.ts`: *"the vocabulary is the authority's, differs per form and per
state, and an enum here would be this software deciding what a state licenses."* That
reasoning is sound for a box a form PRINTS AS TEXT, which is what its neighbour
`inspector_license_type` is.

It is not sound for this box. The 1802 does not print the qualification as text; it prints
six checkboxes, and the field map has one `whenValue` per box. The profile input's
placeholder reads *"e.g. Building code inspector"*, and the form needs the string
`building_code_inspector`.

**This does not print a blank box; it refuses.** `checkChoicesAreReachable` compares the
answer against the map's own boxes before a document exists and fails by name:
`"inspector_qualification" was answered "Building code inspector" and this form has no box
for that answer (it has: home_inspector, …)`. So the failure is loud, and the placeholder
is teaching the value that causes it.

**To close it:** the profile field needs to offer this form's six categories rather than a
free text box — the values are the form's own, and the labels are the six sentences the
page prints (*Home inspector licensed under Section 468.8314, Florida Statutes, …*).
Whatever shape that takes has to keep the free-text case working for the forms that print a
qualification as text, so it is a decision rather than a one-line change. **Until then, an
inspector producing this form has to type the underscored token exactly**, and the produce
was verified that way: `home_inspector` typed into the profile ticks the first box on
page 5.

### 2.2 The single-tick boxes are `multi_select` with one choice, not `boolean`

Eight blanks on this form are one printed box with no alternative: the seven `No
Information Provided for Compliance` cells in the 4.1 table, and *check here if entire roof
deck underside covered*. The map's `whenValue` for each is the string `true`.

An attribute of type `boolean` looks like the obvious carrier and is a trap. The editor's
attribute panel writes `e.target.checked`, so **unticking stores `false`, not nothing** —
`asValue(false)` is the string `"false"`, the form has no box for it, and the produce
refuses. An inspector who ticks a box and changes their mind makes the form unproducible.
Worse for the underside box, which is conditional: `false` counts as answered, so
`applyDependencies` refuses it as an answer to a question the form did not ask.

A `multi_select` with the single choice `true` has the right empty state: the panel writes
`null` when nothing is ticked, which resolves to the empty string, which is what "not
ticked" means everywhere else on this form.

⚠️ The consequence a reader should see: the editor labels that checkbox `true`, because a
multi-select renders its stored values as its labels. That is the same thing every other
statutory template does (`cracking`, `circuit_breaker`, `asphalt_fiberglass_shingle`), and
the printed wording is on the attribute's own name above it.

### 2.3 Two more questions look conditional and are deliberately not declared

`dependsOn` carries exactly the three rules the signed candidate's `fieldDependencies`
carries, and no fourth. Two blanks on this page have the same shape and are NOT declared:

| field | printed under | what happens today |
|---|---|---|
| `opening_protection_wood_panel_type` | answer **C** of question 9 only (*All glazed openings are covered with: □ Plywood OR □ OSB*) | it can be answered while the question above says A, and both boxes print |
| `sealed_roof_deck_method` | answer **A** of question 8 only (the four methods are indented under *A. Sealed Roof Deck*) | the same, for question 8 |

`StatutoryFieldDependency`'s own comment predicted this: *"a fourth that nobody noticed
would look exactly like this."* They are left undeclared because the candidate is a signed
artifact and its dependency set is what was signed; extending it is a modelling decision,
not a template edit. **Declaring either is a one-line change here** once it is ruled on.

⚠️ The first one was SEEN, not reasoned about. A form produced 2026-08-30 answering
question 9 with `X` prints page 5 with the `C.` box empty and **`OSB` ticked underneath
it** — a page that says the glazed openings have no protection and are covered with wood
structural panels. Nothing refused it.

⚠️ Declaring `sealed_roof_deck_method` has a second consequence and it is why this is not
obvious: it is itself the controlling field of one of the three declared rules, and
`applicability.ts` states plainly that **a chain is not supported** — rules are applied in
declaration order against one values object. So that one needs the dependency ORDER the
file describes, not just a rule.

### 2.4 The five "which of these" lists under question 6 are single-choice

Question 6 prints four answers with sub-lists (`A. Toenails`, `B. Clips`, `C. Single
Wraps`, `D. Double Wraps`) plus the minimal-conditions list, and every sub-list joins its
entries with `OR`. They are modelled as `select`, one answer each.

The reasoning is the ruling's own, applied to the same grammar: the minimal conditions were
ruled single-choice because the page reads *"All verified connections are **either**"*, and
question 6 asks for the **WEAKEST** connection — one connection, one basis.

⚠️ The page prints them as checkboxes, so it physically permits more than one, and nothing
has ruled on the four sub-lists. If a house genuinely shows two bases for the weakest
connection, this template cannot record both. Changing a `select` to a `multi_select` here
is a one-line change and does not move a coordinate.

### 2.5 The chart takes several answers per column; the question below takes one

`opening_protection_chart_*` are six `multi_select`s, because the chart prints *"Place an
'X' in each row to identify **all** forms of protection in use for each opening type."* The
question below prints *"Check only one answer below (N/A through Z)"*, so
`opening_protection` is a `select` — and it has to be, because it is the controlling field
of a `dependsOn` rule and `applies()` refuses a list controller by name.

⚠️ The four glazed columns carry **eight** answers and the two non-glazed columns **nine**.
The `D` row is greyed out for the glazed columns on the page, and the map records that as
two fewer boxes. The choice lists here were taken from the map, so they inherit it.

### 2.6 The printed blanks are reproduced in the descriptions; the wording is the page's

Each question's item description carries the form's own option text, because the stored
values are bare letters and a dropdown offering `A B C D` tells an inspector nothing. The
underscore runs the page prints (`Year Built _________.`) are kept, so the description
reads as the page reads.

Measured 2026-08-30 against the PDF: **169 transcribed lines appear on the page verbatim**;
16 more are composed of printed parts, all of which appear (an attribute name that joins a
letter to a row, because the form prints three identically worded permit-date rows and only
the letter above them says which is which — the same problem the four-point form's piping
ages have); 16 lines are this template's own voice and are marked as such.

⚠️ **The four-point form's wording must not be reused here and neither may this form's be
reused there.** Nothing is shared between the two: this is a different authority.

### 2.7 The retrofit sentence has a screen now

The candidate carries exactly one `fieldInstructions` entry, and it is the sentence the
page prints above the minimal conditions:

> Minimal conditions to qualify for categories B, C, or D. All verified connections are
> either: (Note: All retrofit connectors, or structural fasteners, must meet option 3).

It is the description of `question_6_minimal_conditions`, verbatim and first, so an
inspector reading that question reads the Note before choosing. The candidate also records
`showVerbatimBesideAnswers: ["1", "2"]`; the editor has no per-choice hint, so the whole
sentence sits above all three where it applies to all three.

📌 The form asks nothing about whether a connector was retrofitted. Adding a box for it
would put a question to an inspector that the authority never asked, so the Note is shown
and the judgement is left where the page leaves it.

## 3. What is deliberately NOT a gap

- **No `ratingSystem`.** Thirty-four choice questions over seventy distinct values, and the
  values are the form's own letters. TREC gets one embedded rating because all 41 of its
  items ask the same question; here one rating system per question would be the mechanism
  used backwards.
- **No `groups`.** The form prints no repeated block with named slots. The seven roof
  covering rows are seven different coverings, not seven slots of one — the page names each
  one and a house with two roof coverings ticks two named rows.
- **`inspector_printed_name` and `inspector_name` are the same person, twice.** Page 5
  prints *Qualified Inspector Name:* and page 6 opens *I, __________, am a qualified
  inspector*. Both read `inspection.inspector_name`; they are two blanks for one fact, and
  the form prints both.
- **`inspector_phone` reads the company's phone.** The page prints it inside the
  *Inspection Company:* block, under the heading `Phone:`. It is the company's number, and
  `company_phone` is where this product keeps it — the field name is the map's.
- **Page 6's fraud warnings and the closing definition.** Prose, no box and no blank,
  confirmed by rasterising the page rather than inferred from the map stopping.
